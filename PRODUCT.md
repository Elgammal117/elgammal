# Product

## Register
brand

## Users
The primary reader is a **recruiter at a staffing agency**, scanning portfolios at 10:30am, having already reviewed 20 others that day. They open the site in a bright office, on a laptop or large monitor, with email and Slack competing for attention. Mood: scanning, slightly jaded, looking for stack fit and a clear signal of competence. They have 10 seconds before deciding to forward to a client or skip.

## Product Purpose
The portfolio exists to make a recruiter pause and think: **"Wait, this isn't a template."** The single fact that must be unmistakable by the fold: **"The portfolio is the work."** The site itself demonstrates the aesthetic claim. The scroll, the transitions, the resolved details are the proof, not just words on a page.

## Brand Personality
**Three words:** calm, deliberate, aesthetic.
**Archetype:** the engineer, with one explicit inversion: bad at being lazy. The engineer archetype is usually trusted to ship but not to make things beautiful. This engineer breaks that expectation by treating polish and craft as non-negotiable, not optional.

**Motion:** structure-revealing, subtle, technical, every animation has a job. One expressive moment, the signature entrance, carries the personality. The rest of the site stays calm and deliberate.

## Anti-references
1. Dark mode + neon green/cyan + monospace everywhere — the "hacker terminal" template. Triggers template-skip reflex.
2. Three equal project cards in a row, identical sizing, hover lift — the "card grid" trap. Shows zero thought about hierarchy.
3. Circular headshot + "Hi, I'm X, Flutter developer" + skill pills — stock photo energy, no point of view.
4. Gradient text on the name — trust-breaking move, signals template.
5. "I'm a passionate developer who loves building innovative solutions..." opening — trust-breaking move, signals fill-in-the-blank.

## Design Principles
1. Restrained everywhere, one committed moment. The signature entrance is the only expressive animation; everything else is structure-revealing.
2. Engineer-grade polish, not designer-grade expression. The pause comes from refinement, not rebellion.
3. The portfolio IS the work. Every section has to perform at the level of the claim.
4. Light surface, deep ink, one signal accent. Warm cream / off-white paper (no pure white), deep neutral ink (no pure black), single committed accent used sparingly. Same rules as a dark steel+phosphor system, translated.
5. No hidden nav, no scroll-jacking, no scroll-snap-everywhere. The portfolio is scanned, not explored. Recruiter-utility over designer-ego.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
- WCAG AA contrast minimum on the chosen light palette.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` respected; the signature entrance degrades to a calm fade.
- Keyboard-navigable, semantic HTML, copy affordances on contact info.
- Recruiter-friendly: legible on a laptop in a bright office at 10:30am.
